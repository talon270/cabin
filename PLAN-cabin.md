## Context

You want a signalling game on the pattern of `twr`: you work the signals in and around New Delhi station (NDLS), set routes for trains that arrive and depart, and the interlocking refuses anything unsafe. It sits beside the `rails` page and reads the same rule ledger; it replaces nothing.

**The smallest version you said you would play for a week is the real yard with a full shift**: NDLS from the map source, traffic at a researched rate, delay scoring and bests. Slices B0 to B8 build that. B9 ships it.

**Stack: Rust simulation compiled to WebAssembly, one offline HTML file.** You chose it in this session; it is the `twr` pattern and the `GUIDE-games.md` default.

## Phase 0 — shape

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who opens it, when | You, in shifts of 15 to 30 minutes, replayed by seed. Speed of control matters more than onboarding. |
| The one thing it must never get wrong | **The interlocking.** A signal never clears onto a conflicting route, an occupied track or unlocked points (`research.md` C3-139). One function decides every clearance. |

## Part A — risks, ranked (no code exists, so these are build risks)

### A1 · DESIGN RISK (high): the yard graph is unmeasured
The query of 2026-11-14 over `28.632,77.210,28.652,77.228` returned 185 track ways (45 main, 84 yard, 37 siding, 19 crossover), 79 `railway=switch` nodes, 4 `railway_crossing` nodes, 41 buffer stops and 16 platform ways. It asked for tags only. Whether those ways join into one graph, whether each switch has three legs, and whether every platform can be reached from every approach line is **not known**. `twr` found 4 near-miss dead ends and one stand-less pocket at IGI; a rail yard has less slack than an apron.
**Fix:** B0 builds the extractor first and prints a report before any game code exists. A gate fails the extract unless every platform track is reachable from every portal in the direction of travel. This is the smallest fix because it measures the risk with the tool the game needs anyway.

### A2 · DESIGN RISK (high): an interlocking derived from geometry can have a hole
No route table for NDLS is public, so routes come from path search over the graph. 79 points give many signal-to-signal paths, and a missed conflict (a shared diamond, a fouled leg, an overlap that crosses another route) lets two trains meet.
**Fix:** one function, `Interlocking::request`, is the only writer of the lock table. A separate invariant checker, which knows nothing about locks and works from train positions alone, runs after every tick in tests. B5 soaks 10 game hours on 3 seeds and fuzzes 1,000,000 random actions. The checker is separate on purpose: `rails/README.md` says "a simulator checked against its own code proves nothing".

### A3 · MODEL GAP (high): OpenStreetMap holds 2 signals for the whole station
Three searches found no public NDLS signalling plan, yard diagram or station working rules. "Not found" is the claim, not "does not exist".
**Fix:** signals are generated by rule and tagged `authored`: a Home outside all connections on each approach line (C3-58), a Starter at each platform end protecting the first facing points or fouling mark (C3-63). `data/ndls.overrides.toml` moves, adds or names a signal, as `twr`'s `vidp.overrides.toml` does, so a real position can replace a generated one when a source turns up. B1 runs one more search.

### A4 · MODEL GAP (medium): the rules read are not Northern Railway's
`research.md` read the Subsidiary Rules of Southern, Western, South Central and Central Railway. NDLS is on Northern Railway. The General Rules apply everywhere; every speed and every zonal practice does not.
**Fix:** the game uses General Rules rows as `solid` constants and takes Southern's figures (15 km/h and 50 km/h over facing points, C3-49 and C3-52) as `assumed` starting values. The "what is real" panel says so in one line. Reading NR's rulebook is out of scope.

### A5 · DESIGN RISK (medium): a true-scale yard cannot meet the 44 px target rule
The station is about 2 km long and its 16 platform tracks sit a few metres apart. At 1920 px a true-scale view puts neighbouring tracks, and so neighbouring signals, a few pixels apart. The hit-slop card needs 44 × 44 px targets that do not overlap.
**Fix:** the panel rotates the yard to its long axis and stretches the cross-track direction by `PANEL_STRETCH` (assumed, starting at 6). Real control panels are schematic for the same reason. The page says "not to scale across the tracks". Verification counts signal pairs closer than 44 px at the default view and wants 0.

### A6 · DESIGN RISK (medium): the pace may be wrong
Real trains dwell for minutes and cross a throat slowly. Real time may be dull; compressed time may be frantic.
**Fix:** `TIME_SCALE` is one tagged constant. The B5 soak prints decisions per real minute at three scales before any control UI exists, and you pick the value from those numbers.

### A7 · INCONSISTENCY (low): the look's one red is already spoken for
Nothing OS Classic spends one red per screen. The `rails` rule is that red, yellow and green appear only as signal aspects.
**Fix:** aspect colours appear in signal heads and nowhere else. A set route is a bright line, an unset track a dim one, a train a solid block with its number. Refusals are plain text with a glyph. The look loses its accent; that is the cost.

### A8 · NOISE (low): three facts to carry
- Press and Wikipedia credit NDLS with the largest route relay interlocking; Guinness files the record under "Delhi Main Station", February 1999. The game prints no record claim.
- Old Delhi (DLI) is unmeasured: Overpass answered "at capacity" on three tries. NDLS is first for that reason only.
- On this machine `node` and Playwright have been broken since 2026-11-12. The workaround is in the `twr` memory note (`PLAYWRIGHT_NODEJS_PATH`, cached Chromium 1248).

## Architecture

### 1 · The sketch

- **Things.** *Yard*: tracks, points, crossings, signals, platforms, portals; owned by `data/ndls.yard`, written only by the extractor. *Rules*: owned by `rails/research.md`; the game holds constants that cite row ids. *Traffic profile*: hourly rates, mix, train pool; owned by `data/ndls.traffic.toml`, written from `research/traffic.md`. *Train*, *Route lock*, *Shift* (seed, action list, three scores): sim state, never stored. *Bests* and theme: `localStorage` key `cabin.v1`.
- **Path 1, arrival.** A train is offered at a portal and runs to the Home. You click the Home, then a platform. `Interlocking::request` checks the route. Points lock, the Home clears, the train runs, each section releases behind its tail, the train stops at the berth.
- **Path 2, departure.** The dwell ends. You click the Starter, then a portal. The same function checks the route and the line beyond the portal. The train leaves; its lateness is scored.
- **Path 3, refusal.** A request that fails changes nothing. The panel prints the reason and names the route, train or point in the way.
- **Edges.** OpenStreetMap (build time). Timetable research (build time). `rails/research.md` (test time). The seed; no clock read in the sim. `localStorage`. A second open tab.

### 3 · Guards

**Guard 1: no signal clears onto an unsafe route.**
- **The one function:** `Interlocking::request(&mut self, yard, occupancy, entry, exit) -> Result<RouteId, Refusal>` in `crates/sim/src/interlock.rs`. The lock table is a private field of that module. `request` is its only inserter; `release_behind` and `cancel` only remove. Trains take movement authority from `Interlocking::aspect` and from nothing else.
- **Worst inputs, each a named test:**
  1. Two routes over one diamond crossing requested in the same tick.
  2. Two arrivals routed into one platform from opposite ends.
  3. A route cancelled in the face of a train, then a conflicting route requested at once.
  4. A train longer than its berth, its tail left across the points behind.
  5. A route in rear requested while the train ahead straddles three sections.
  6. 1,000,000 random requests, cancels and ticks over 3 seeds, with the invariant checker after every tick.
- **The invariant checker** (`tests/common/invariant.rs`) computes each train's body interval from its position and length and asserts: no two bodies share a section, a point or a crossing; no body stands within the fouling length of a leg another train occupies; every moving train holds a lock on the resource ahead of its head; no locked point changes its lie.

**Guard 2: a bad extract never replaces a good yard.** `cabin-osm` writes `data/ndls.yard.tmp`, runs the gate, and renames only on a pass. Worst input: an Overpass answer with HTTP 200 and no ways (the case `twr/fetch-osm.sh` already catches), and an extract in which one crossover way has been deleted.

**Guard 3: saved bests survive.** A corrupt or future-version `cabin.v1` is kept under `cabin.v1.corrupt`, a persistent inline banner says so, and the page runs on defaults. Worst input: `cabin.v1` set to `{"schema":99}` and to `not json`.

### Interfaces the slices share

```rust
// yard.rs
pub struct Yard {
    pub osm_base: String,            // extract date, printed in the panel
    pub nodes: Vec<Node>,            // position in metres from the origin
    pub edges: Vec<Edge>,            // one track section: two nodes, a polyline, a length, a Kind
    pub points: Vec<Point>,          // stem, normal leg, reverse leg
    pub crossings: Vec<Crossing>,    // two straight pairs through one node
    pub signals: Vec<Signal>,        // kind, edge, offset, direction faced, Origin
    pub platforms: Vec<Platform>,    // ref, edge, usable length
    pub portals: Vec<Portal>,        // where a running line leaves the extract
}
pub enum Kind { Running, Yard, Siding }
pub enum Origin { Generated, Override, Osm }
impl Yard { pub fn parse(text: &str) -> Result<Yard, YardError>; }

// route.rs
pub enum Exit { Berth(PlatformId, End), Portal(PortalId) }
pub enum Res { Edge(EdgeId), Point(PointId), Crossing(CrossingId) }
pub enum Lie { Normal, Reverse }
pub struct Route {
    pub id: RouteId, pub entry: SignalId, pub exit: Exit,
    pub path: Vec<EdgeId>, pub points: Vec<(PointId, Lie)>,
    pub overlap: Vec<EdgeId>,          // OVERLAP_M beyond the exit signal
    pub holds: BTreeSet<Res>,          // path + points + crossings + overlap + fouled legs
}
pub fn build_routes(yard: &Yard) -> Vec<Route>;   // shortest path per (entry, exit)

// interlock.rs
pub enum Aspect { Red, Yellow, DoubleYellow, Green }
pub enum Refusal {
    NoSuchRoute,
    Conflict { with: RouteId, at: Res },
    Occupied { at: Res, by: TrainId },
    OverlapOccupied { at: Res, by: TrainId },
    NoLineClear { portal: PortalId, wait_s: u32 },
    TrainPastSignal { train: TrainId },
    ApproachLocked { release_in_s: u32 },
}
impl Interlocking {
    pub fn request(&mut self, yard: &Yard, occ: &Occupancy, entry: SignalId, exit: Exit) -> Result<RouteId, Refusal>;
    pub fn cancel(&mut self, yard: &Yard, occ: &Occupancy, route: RouteId) -> Result<(), Refusal>;
    pub fn release_behind(&mut self, yard: &Yard, occ: &Occupancy);
    pub fn aspect(&self, yard: &Yard, signal: SignalId) -> Aspect;
}

// train.rs
pub struct Occupancy { /* per train: the edges its body covers, head and tail offsets */ }
impl Occupancy { pub fn holder(&self, r: Res) -> Option<TrainId>; }

// traffic.rs
pub struct Profile { /* parsed data/ndls.traffic.toml: rates per portal, mix, train pool */ }
pub enum Mode { Practice, Light, Normal, Peak }
pub enum End { Up, Down }   // which end of a platform

// lib.rs
pub enum Action { SetRoute { entry: SignalId, exit: Exit }, CancelRoute(RouteId), Pause(bool), EndShift }
impl World {
    pub fn new(yard: Yard, profile: Profile, mode: Mode, seed: u64) -> World;
    pub fn apply(&mut self, a: Action) -> Result<(), Refusal>;
    pub fn step_ticks(&mut self, n: u32);
}
```

### The interlocking rules, with their ledger rows

| Rule in `request` | Row | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| A signal shows its most restrictive aspect until a route is set | C3-137 | solid |
| Before it clears: facing points set and locked, trailing points set, line clear | C3-139 | solid |
| The line is also clear for `OVERLAP_M` = 120 m beyond the exit signal | C3-142, C3-143 | solid for multiple-aspect signals; that NDLS is multiple-aspect is assumed |
| With two trains approaching, a signal clears for a second only where the interlocking makes it safe: disjoint `holds` | C3-154 | solid |
| A departure needs the line beyond the portal free (stands in for Line Clear) | C3-148 | the rule solid; `BLOCK_HEADWAY_S` assumed |
| A cleared signal is not put back before the whole train has passed, except by `cancel` | C3-134 | solid |
| `cancel` with a train on the approach holds the route for `APPROACH_RELEASE_S` | none | assumed |
| A failure leaves the signal at red | C3-133 | solid |
| Points rest normal when no route holds them | C3-164 | solid |

**Aspects are derived on every tick, never stored**, as in `rails/js/sim.js`: red with no route; yellow when the exit signal is red; double yellow when the exit signal is yellow; green otherwise. The ledger does not settle the off aspect of a Starter (`rails/README.md`), so that row is tagged `assumed` in the panel.

### Design: Nothing OS Classic, dark

- **Look.** `md/designs.md § Nothing OS, two styles › Classic`: `--bg #000`, `--fg #fff`, `--dim #8a8a8e`, `--line #2a2a2c` in dark, the home theme; the light pair from the same entry. 1 px hairlines, 8 px radius, uppercase mono labels at 11 px. **Sourcing is weak:** the entry tags these values `blog`. No installed style skill carries this look; `mono` is the nearest and is not used. The look's red is dropped (A7).
- **Colour rule.** Red, yellow and green are defined in the signal-head draw function and used nowhere else. `tools/verify_b8.py` greps the built page for the aspect hex values outside that function.
- **Type.** `--mono: ui-monospace, "JetBrains Mono", "DejaVu Sans Mono", monospace` and `--sans: "Liberation Sans", Arial, sans-serif`, the stacks `twr/web/shell.html` names. No web font; the page stays offline.
- **Layout.** The panel takes about two thirds of the width and the full height under a header. A rack on the right holds three bays: due, at platform, ready to depart. The header carries the clock and the three scores. Checked at 1920 px and 1500 px. Phone width is out of scope.
- **Motion: Precise product, overridden by the look to 100 ms linear.** Control presses invert fill and text in 100 ms, no bounce. Aspects change on the tick, as LED heads do. Trains move because the sim moves them. Pan and zoom use the rAF spring copied from `twr/web/shell.html` with `MOTION.gesture` (k 380, ζ 0.75) and `MOTION.rubberBand` 0.55 at the edges, from `designs.md § Motion tokens`. No looping animation, no entrance animation. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, pan and zoom jump and nothing else moves by animation.
- **Sound.** None in this plan.

- **Fouling is checked per request.** A route is refused when another train stands within `FOULING_M` of a junction on it, on an edge the route does not use. This is how `OverlapOccupied` happens in NDLS: no Starter stands on another route's overlap track (counted: 0).
